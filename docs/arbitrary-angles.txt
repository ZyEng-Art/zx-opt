Mixed-angle inputs with conditional Clifford+T rewrites

Run with the existing pinned Python/PyZX environment from docs/runbook.txt:

$PYTHON -m zxopt.general_search runs/mixed-angles --input tests/fixtures/mixed_angles.qasm --allow-arbitrary-angles --jpv --policy online --seconds 60 --max-steps 128 --rollout 32

general_campaign and target_campaign also forward --allow-arbitrary-angles
to every run, including target preflight. A short frozen/online integration
check (not an optimization-efficacy study) can use:

$PYTHON -m zxopt.general_campaign runs/mixed-angle-pair --input tests/fixtures/mixed_angles.qasm --allow-arbitrary-angles --arms jpv_frozen jpv_online --seeds 11 --seconds 60 --workers 2

Input and representation
- The option accepts numerical rotation angles supported by the pinned QASM
  parser. It preserves their parsed rational coefficients of pi instead of
  snapping to the nearest pi/4. The fixture includes pi/3, pi/8 and 0.3 radians.
- Rational multiples such as pi/3 remain rational phases. Decimal radians pass
  through PyZX's existing numerical-to-rational parser; this is not exact
  symbolic arithmetic for arbitrary real numbers or unbound parameters.
- General diagrams and serialized proof objects record allow_arbitrary=true.
  Clone, contextual substitution, tensor product, H-H cancellation and macro
  transitions preserve this mode. Historical CT records and keys are unchanged.
- Without the option, the original strict pi/4 input contract is retained.

Conditional action matching
- The finite JPV proposal menu still instantiates only Clifford+T templates.
  If an internal matched spider has a non-pi/4 phase, that candidate is skipped.
  Its angle is never truncated, rounded or deleted to make a match succeed.
- Arbitrary-angle spiders outside the matched region remain as its context;
  CT rules can therefore optimize suitable subgraphs of a mixed-angle circuit.
- This conservative menu also skips S/H on non-pi/4 spiders, although those
  mathematical schemas permit arbitrary phases. Generalizing their trusted
  template registry is separate work.
- Existing native/macro rules and scoring simplifications keep their own angle
  conditions. They can perform valid generic operations involving arbitrary
  phases. These phases are preserved semantically, not frozen against every rule.

Verification and scoring
- Every unique raw graph and normalized scoring view retains the existing full
  fixed-wire operator check against the original Qiskit circuit (tolerance 2e-8).
  Every unique scored QASM is checked independently; invalid extraction results
  remain quarantined, with no cost or reward credit.
- Named wires and amplitudes are preserved. Only a unit-modulus overall phase
  may be aligned, exactly as in prior experiments.
- The CT rule registry and C8 exact_tensor still reject non-pi/4 phases. The
  general representation must never cause int(phase*4) truncation in either
  the exact evaluator or the compiled finite-phase matcher.
- Total NAM gates remain the objective, using h/rz/x/cx. An arbitrary-angle rz
  counts as one gate; it is not compiled into an approximate Clifford+T sequence.
- Legal graphs without a valid extracted circuit may continue as before.

Scope
This adds mixed-angle input support, not an arbitrary-angle complete axiom set
or guaranteed extraction. CT completeness and finite-search limitations stay
as described in docs/clifford-t-completeness.txt and docs/general-search.txt.

Checks
tests/test_arbitrary_angles.py checks import/export and scalar phases, all 11
axiom families in both colors/directions in non-CT contexts, proof replay,
phase-signature rejection, strict-mode preservation, every generated action's
operator and native-preview agreement on the mixed-angle fixture.
